Self-hosted executive assistant

The assistant you would hire, if you could afford one.

Tacitus takes the friction out of the places a human executive assistant would be great: the inbox, the calendar, the forms, the follow-ups, the files. It reads your mail and calendars, drafts in your voice, finds meeting times on your real calendars, and tracks what you owe to whom. You talk to it in a private chat.

A human EA cannot make decisions on behalf of the executive. Neither can Tacitus. That is the design principle: nothing is sent, booked or deleted until you press the button.

Scheduling

Scheduling is a sentence.

A constraint solver reads Outlook and every Google calendar you name, applies your rules, and proposes times. The same solver fills When2Meet, Doodle and Calendly polls. The invite waits behind a Create button.

  • Your rules are recurring events on a calendar you edit in your own calendar app: working hours, lunch, no-meeting Fridays, who gets priority.
  • Reschedule requests are detected in mail within minutes and staged as a button.
  • Attendee free/busy comes from their calendars, not from the model's memory.

Inbox

One message. Ten drafts. Zero tabs.

Dictate your verdicts on the day's mail in one breath. Each reply is drafted in the voice you use with that person, with proposed times where a meeting was asked for, and a Send card lands in your DM. Sending closes the ledger item and archives the thread.

  • Style profiles are learned from 90 days of your sent mail: greeting, closing, length, formality, per contact.
  • A yes to a meeting always carries two or three solver times.
  • Every draft names the ledger row it closes.

Letters, forms, spreadsheets

Dictate it. Get a PDF on letterhead.

Recommendation letters from a voice memo, sponsor forms from a web link, health forms from a photo of the blank. It compiles on your letterhead with your signature, checks every page, and tells you exactly what it left blank.

  • Letters are LaTeX on your template, kept with their source, revised in place.
  • It reads what the form actually asks for before it fills it.
  • Your CV lives in Overleaf. It pulls, edits and pushes it.

Working documents

Review it like code. Comment on any line.

Outlines, briefs, memos and proposal sections open as pull requests in a private GitHub repository, one sentence per line. You leave inline comments and suggested wording on your phone. The assistant applies the whole review in one commit, replies on each thread, and resolves it. You merge.

  • The merged file is the finished document.
  • Confidential material never goes there: letters, reviews, personnel files stay local.
  • A comment it cannot resolve stays open with its question.

Rules and closure

Say it once. In your own words.

A preference you state in chat is recorded verbatim, scoped, and applied in every later briefing and draft. Obligations close themselves from evidence: your reply, your archive, a matching calendar event, a send. If you ever have to say "that's done", it logs its own miss as a defect to fix.

  • Standing rules outrank the built-in skills.
  • Thin evidence becomes a one-tap "Looks done?" card instead of a silent close.
  • List mail and cold applicant mail are digested in code, never surfaced one by one.

Models

Frontier intelligence. Your mail stays home.

Claude Code or Codex does the judgment: the conversation, the hard reply, the plan. If you have a GPU, an open-weight model you host reads every message first, writes the digests, the tags, the briefings and the first drafts. The frontier model sees summaries of what needs you, and reads one thread's text only when a request calls for it. Nothing is read in bulk.

🔒One-time tokens. Sending mail, creating or moving an event, a deferred send, a delete, a publish, a reviewer invite: each needs a token the bot mints only when you press the card's button. The model holds no chat credential, so it cannot press it.
👤A separate user. The model runs as its own Unix account. Your mail and calendar tokens belong to you and are unreadable to it. Reads and drafts go through a mediator that runs as you and refuses to send.
📕Read-only code. The rules it runs under cannot be edited by the model. Changes come from your checkout and nowhere else.
🏠Your chat on your server. Zulip on your own machine over your tailnet is the recommended front end. Slack works if you accept that Slack stores the conversation.

Judgment

Claude Code, or Codex

Runs every conversation, every project room, the audits and the scheduling channel. Claude Code is the workhorse in production today; Codex runs under evaluation on the same lanes with every write recorded.

Mail front desk, optional

A model you host

Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint over https: vLLM, Ollama, or a server your group runs. The reference install uses a 27B open-weight model on a colleague's machine at the same university. It reads the inbox, classifies, summarizes, tags, drafts and briefs. Money, contracts, deans, declines and personnel never run here.

No model at all

Plain Python

Reading the inbox, merging calendars, solving for meeting times, closing ledger items from evidence, learning your style from sent mail. Most of the work, and it spends no tokens.

Install

An always-on Linux or macOS box, and about an hour.

Start with the email profile: triage, the ledger, drafts in your voice, meeting times in the morning report. Move to assistant when you want the chat rooms and the buttons.
